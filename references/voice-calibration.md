# Voice calibration

Use this file when the user asks for a specific voice or gives writing samples.

## What to preserve

Look for:
- sentence length range
- punctuation habits
- contractions or no contractions
- favorite plain words
- level of directness
- amount of humor or irritation
- how the writer expresses uncertainty
- whether the writer uses fragments
- how formal the writer is with greetings and closings

Mirror the pattern, not the exact phrases.

## What not to fake

Do not invent:
- personal stories
- credentials
- emotional history
- sensory details
- quotes
- statistics
- named examples
- regional slang the user has not shown

If the text needs a real detail, use a bracketed placeholder such as `[specific example from your team]` or write a version that works without the detail.

## Default voice when none is given

Use plain, competent prose:
- direct claim first
- concrete nouns
- active verbs
- moderate sentence variation
- few adjectives
- minimal formatting
- no performance of warmth
- no theatrical contrast

## Platform notes

LinkedIn:
- Avoid fake humility, grand career lessons, and comment-bait endings.
- Keep the first line specific.
- Use short paragraphs, but not one-sentence drama stacks.

Email:
- Be useful fast.
- Do not over-explain context the recipient already has.
- Make the ask visible.

Website copy:
- Replace benefits like "save time" with the exact saved step.
- Avoid "seamless," "powerful," and "all-in-one" unless supported by proof.

Executive memo:
- Start with the decision or recommendation.
- Put the decision or recommendation first.
- Use numbers and owners where available.

Academic prose:
- Keep source-backed uncertainty precise.
- Avoid trying to sound casual.
- Prefer precision over personality.
- Preserve citations.

Creative/nonfiction:
- Keep idiosyncrasy.
- Do not make the prose uniformly smooth.
- Specific images beat generic emotion.
